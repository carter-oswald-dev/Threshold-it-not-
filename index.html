Toner printers don't do gradients. Left to a plain black-and-white threshold, most photos collapse into blobs like the one shown here.

So instead we use dithering, which tricks your eyes into seeing gray scale while only using black and white.

But different images work better with different types of dithering. Using just the common types, you'd have to manually check up to 23 versions to see which one looks best.

Instead of wasting time doing that by hand, this site can decide for you — using a simple custom-trained neural network, similar to the kind post offices use to read handwritten addresses and sort packages.

The model looks at your submitted image and runs entirely in your browser, so there's no privacy concern — nothing is uploaded anywhere.

It then ranks every dithering option from best to worst for your image.

You can download the top pick, browse and choose one yourself, or bulk-download everything to sort through by eye on your own device.

If you want to see it in action, try the solar eclipse photo from the top of the page as a demo.
